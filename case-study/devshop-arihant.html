01 · The thesis

Not another AI agency. An operating partner.

Software is easy to add. Improving how a business actually operates is the hard part — and the only part worth paying for.

I start with the business, not the tool. Every problem is broken down the same way, so the answer is specific rather than generic:

ABusiness area
→
BFunction
→
CProcess
→
DTask

Then each task is decided on its own merits — human, software, automation, AI, or human + AI — never automated by default. That decomposition runs on PTM™ — Process, Training, Mapping: what work happens, what your team needs to know, and who does each part.

The line that doesn't move

Machines do the work. Money, people and promises stay with a human.

Every task is classified Manual, Semi-Automated, Automated or Fully AI-driven, and every AI action is scoped to Execute, Recommend or Assist against a human who decides or reviews.

04 · How we engage

Scope before price. Complexity sets the resource.

No one-size-fits-all monthly technology fee. Commercials reflect real effort, and are agreed before any work begins.

EngagementForCommercials
1 · Custom Build A specific problem needing bespoke technology / AI / automation. Build priced per scope — agreed first; payment secures the slot and starts the build clock. Then a monthly fee to keep it running and improving. Genuinely complex systems are human-priced after a short scoping call.
2 · Retail OS Consumer brands that want an operating layer, run for them. ₹5,000 deposit — fully adjusted against actual onboarding tech cost; DevShop keeps none; no other upfront fee. Then one model: profit share ~40% of the profit pool (standard, can come down), revenue share 15–20%, or a retainer from ₹2.5L / month.
3 · Combination A brand needing bespoke functionality alongside the operating system. Scoped to the actual requirement — Custom Build commercials for the build, Retail OS commercials for the operating layer.

Pre-revenue founders can enter Retail OS on an AI-enabler track: DevShop's return is a share of the brand IP rather than a profit-pool split. Anything outside an agreed scope is discussed and priced separately, not absorbed silently.

Commercial principles
  • Scope before price.
  • Complexity sets resources — hours behind the price, named.
  • Human vs. AI decided per task, in the open.
  • Direct technology costs are transparent; ad spend at cost.
  • Support reflects real operating need.
  • Agreed before work starts; material changes re-scoped.
  • Delivery economics are measured, so future pricing is evidence-based.
