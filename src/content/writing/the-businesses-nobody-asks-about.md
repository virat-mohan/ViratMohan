The clean version of my career goes: audit, restaurants, cloud kitchens, capital, now AI. Linear, sensible, a straight line you could draw on a slide. It's also missing about half of what actually happened.

**Praxis Technologies** traded computer hardware between the UAE and India — cabinets, mice, keyboards, the unglamorous plumbing of every office that existed before laptops got cheap. It was the first business I ever ran, and the first place I learned that margin is not a detail, it's the whole game. You can move a lot of boxes and still lose money if you don't understand your own costs. I understood mine because I had no choice.

**Perfect Ice** was my father's before it was mine. A D2C ice brand with home delivery — production, cold-chain, last-mile, all under one roof. I revived it rather than founded it, which is its own kind of lesson: inheriting a business teaches you things that starting one from scratch doesn't, mostly about which parts of an operation were actually load-bearing and which just looked that way. Demand spiked on the two hottest days of the year and disappeared the rest. You learn to respect a business with a brutal seasonality curve.

**Delhi on the Go** was a free daily newspaper for metro commuters. Content, print, distribution — and a business model that lived entirely on advertising, which means you learn very quickly whether anyone actually wants what you're printing, because there's no subscription cushion to hide behind.

**Marblex India** manufactured concrete pavers, including for the Commonwealth Games. Heavy industry, machinery, a completely different rhythm from anything else I've done — no software, no brand, just physical output you can drop on your foot.

None of these are on a pitch deck anywhere. They didn't lead to funding rounds or acquisitions. What they gave me instead was range: I've now sat inside a trading desk, a food brand, a media business and a factory, and the thing that's true across all four is the thing I keep coming back to on this site — the businesses that work are the ones where somebody understood the actual mechanics, not the story. Everything after this — the audits, the restaurants, the kitchens, ClarityHQ — was built on instincts these four gave me first.
