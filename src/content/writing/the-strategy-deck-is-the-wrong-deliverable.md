title: "The Strategy Deck Is the Wrong Deliverable"
standfirst: "Why I stopped pitching campaigns and started shipping them the way a franchise ships a store."

We were mid-raise, and every week the business didn't grow was runway we weren't getting back. I needed marketing that shipped, not marketing that got planned.

That quarter I sat through four agency pitches. All four opened the same way: a strategic discovery phase, four to six weeks of workshops, before a single ad ran. All four were staffed by smart people. None could tell me why a business burning cash on a clock needed six weeks of process before the first campaign touched a customer.

Eventually I understood what was actually being sold — not the outcome, the pitch itself. The deck, the workshop, the feeling of bespoke rigour applied specifically to you. There's nothing dishonest about that. It's just misaligned with what a lean, capital-constrained business actually needs, which isn't to feel understood — it's the next thirty days of customer acquisition running by Friday.

I opened the first three Pita Pit stores in India myself. By the time we'd scaled to twenty-one units, not one later opening involved reinventing how a store worked — the menu, the SOPs, the supply chain logic were already decided and written down the first time, properly. What scaled was the deployment, not the thinking. Nobody re-litigates the recipe every time a franchise opens; they deploy it fast, and it still tastes right. A launch calendar or an outbound sequence isn't a bespoke invention each time either, however much the pitch process pretends otherwise — the judgment about what works was already made once. What most businesses are actually buying is that judgment, packaged to deploy on their brand in days.

The objection I hear is that fixed scope means templated, and templated means soulless. But a Pita Pit in Riyadh and one in Gurugram were never identical, and neither should two campaigns built on the same playbook be. What's fixed is the shape and the speed. What's not fixed is the brand truth underneath it — which is exactly why that layer has to be right before anything else happens.

So I stopped selling the workshop. What we ship now is closer to a catalogue than a pitch — a defined set of campaign types, each built to run in days, each drawing from the same brand record instead of reconstructing it from scratch. The strategy deck was never the deliverable. It only felt like one because nobody had built the thing that could replace it.
