Serious, gallery-quality Indian contemporary artists have almost no direct route into European collector demand — the usual path runs through a Western gallery discovering and representing them, which is slow, relationship-gated, and leaves most working artists out entirely. On the other side, European collectors interested in Indian contemporary art have no single trustworthy place to browse vetted, purchasable work with real provenance. This platform is built to be that place — a two-sided marketplace with a full curation layer behind it, connecting artists and collectors directly.

### Discovery, For Collectors

- A browsable catalogue of every available piece — artist, medium, size, year, and price, or "price on request" where an artist wants it negotiated.
- A dedicated page for every artist on the roster — portrait, story, and everything currently available under their name.
- A **sold-works archive**, kept separate from the live catalogue, showing real placed pieces with full detail — not just what's for sale today, but proof of what has actually sold.
- A founder page establishing who is personally curating the roster.
- A journal and press section for editorial coverage.

### Buying, Made Simple

- Every artwork page shows the piece in its true proportions, not squeezed into a fixed frame — a tall painting looks tall, a wide one looks wide.
- An enquiry form built directly onto each artwork's own page, so interest in a specific piece goes straight to the right place, not a generic contact form.
- A collector interested in more than one piece can gather them in one place before enquiring.
- One-tap WhatsApp, live from the site, for collectors and artists who would rather message than email.

### Joining, Made Effortless For Artists

- A full application — details, artwork, agreement — that an artist can complete and submit **without ever creating a password or an account**.
- The legal agreement between the gallery and the artist is presented and signed inside that same application, no separate paperwork step.
- Artists can upload photos of their work and themselves as part of applying, before any account exists for them at all.
- A dedicated page inviting new artists to apply.

### Running the Gallery

- An internal dashboard, separate from the public site, listing every artist and their full body of work.
- Adding an artist checks automatically for a duplicate, offering to add new work to an existing profile instead of creating a second one by mistake.
- Each artwork can be staged privately before it goes live, and marked sold, independently, with a single switch each.
- New work can be added to an artist's profile directly from their card, without leaving the page.
- Every photo can be zoomed to full size for a fast quality check before anything is published.
- Removing an artist cleans up everything connected to them automatically, not just their profile.
- Admin tooling built for day-to-day roster and inventory management.

### A Look Built For The Work

- A full, bespoke visual identity — an editorial cream-and-ink palette with a considered serif and body type pairing, in place of a generic template.
- Navigation rebuilt and tested across real device sizes, from a phone to a large desktop, so nothing ever overflows or hides.
- Consistent image framing across the catalogue, so artwork is shown the way it was meant to be seen.

### Communication, Handled Automatically

- An enquiry on a specific artwork arrives with that artwork and artist's details already attached — never a generic "someone contacted you" notice.
- Newsletter sign-ups captured independently of the enquiry itself.
- Automated contract delivery — the moment an artist's application is approved, the signed agreement is generated and emailed to them, built and wired up end to end.

The part invisible from just browsing is what makes "apply without an account" actually work. Letting someone upload a photo before they have a login, against a database that is otherwise locked down by default, needed a proper secure hand-off built specifically for that moment. Every application still creates a real account behind the scenes, because the system requires one — with a password the artist never sees or needs. And every table in the system is locked down so that an anonymous visitor can only ever see what has been deliberately published, and every change — an enquiry, an application, an admin edit — has to go through a controlled process that checks who is actually allowed to make it.

It carries four represented artists with full profiles today, several works each, and a running sold-works archive with real placed pieces already on record.
