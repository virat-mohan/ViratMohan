How we work it out

  • Energy. Resting energy is 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75 kilocalories a day, the standard formula in the NRC's Nutrient Requirements of Dogs and Cats (2006) and the WSAVA nutrition guidelines.
  • Life stage. The daily need is that figure times a factor: 1.6 for a neutered adult, 1.8 for an intact adult, 3.0 for a puppy under four months, 2.0 for a puppy from four months, as published in Small Animal Clinical Nutrition (Hand et al.). Breed size sets when a dog counts as a puppy or a senior.
  • Body shape. We ask how the ribs and waist feel, the idea behind the 9-point body condition score vets use, because it says more than weight alone.
  • What we leave out. Whatever you tick, we remove. Chicken, dairy, beef and wheat are the foods most often reported as allergens in dogs.
A starting point, not veterinary advice. A vet adjusts these figures for the individual dog. Dogs with a health condition, pregnant or nursing dogs, and dogs gaining or losing weight fast should follow their vet's plan.
