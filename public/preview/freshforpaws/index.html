My recommendation: A, with two borrowed pieces

Why A converts best. The fresh-food leaders sell with a short quiz that turns "what should my dog eat?" into a personal plan. Butternut Box, which runs this playbook, converts about 2.11% of visitors against The Farmer's Dog's 0.7% on far more traffic (The Food Stack). A quiz also collects the pet's details from people who don't buy yet, so I can follow up on WhatsApp and email with the right recipe.

Why it fits Fresh For Paws. Srishti is a certified canine & feline nutritionist, so "a plan made for your dog" is believable from her, and it stays warm and founder-led.

What I'd borrow from the other two: the reel-style customer reviews and #ChooseFreshForPaws wall from C on the recipe pages (proof that dogs actually eat it), and the magazine-style Our Story page from B.

Why not B or C alone: B is beautiful but slower to the first click, which suits a premium global audience more than Delhi NCR parents buying a first pack. C drives shares but leans on a steady stream of customer video Fresh For Paws would need to build first.
